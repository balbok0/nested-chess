Method

Win rate is wins ÷ games. Match score is (wins + ½ draws) ÷ games; above 50% means outscoring the opponent. Intervals treat a color-swapped pair as one observation and use conservative 95% bounds. Draws remain separate from wins.

The 420 pilots produced 840 within-policy/color game-pair comparisons. Median local sequence similarity was 9.8%, allowing board relabeling and global interleaving; the closest pair was 50.9% similar. This measures move overlap, not strategic diversity. Pilot comparisons.

No evaluation-based adjudication or move-limit draws. Games finish under the engine's king-capture, majority, no-progress, and repetition rules.

Budgets, resources, and data

Time budgets are unchanged from the presets. Global search uses all legal moves and a 100,000-node cap. The local baseline chooses a free board using legal_boards[global_ply % count]. Each game uses fresh engine processes.

Confidence bounds use Bonferroni-adjusted Clopper–Pearson intervals for the paired-outcome distribution. They account for pairing and remain nonzero at 0% or 100% observed wins. Bounds are per pairing, not jointly corrected across the matrix. They assume independent, identically distributed pairs; shared machine load can weaken that assumption. They describe these engine runs, not optimal play or Elo. Binomial interval reference.

Fairness

The classical setup is symmetric under exchanging colors and reflecting local and macro ranks. White still gets the first move, so symmetry alone does not establish balance. The equal-difficulty local controls measure color effects for these bots; they do not settle the game's optimal-play result.

Search and evaluation

Rust runs negamax with alpha-beta and iterative deepening. Fairy-Stockfish supplies cached local estimates. Search follows actual global turns and routing, keeping every legal move.

eval = P(match win) + ½ P(match draw)

A dynamic program combines settled points with estimated outcomes for unfinished boards. It accumulates half-point totals, then evaluates the majority threshold. A third board loss on a four-board grid is an exact match loss, regardless of local advantages elsewhere.

Local centipawn scores use a bounded tanh heuristic. Unfinished boards initially have no estimated draw mass except frozen boards; actual draws are exact. Outcomes are assumed independent beyond the search horizon. These are uncalibrated estimates.

The first improvement to test is retaining the best evaluated root move when depth one is interrupted. The current fallback discards that partial work. Higher presets also failed to improve strength consistently, so completing more search alone does not establish a better bot.
